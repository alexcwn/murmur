Murmur  /  Icon Study

Make it an instrument

Four marks taken from inside the app rather than illustrating what it does. Murmur already has a face — brushed bezels, a cream meter, one red lamp — and an icon drawn from that will sit with the app instead of announcing it.

Every one is a real object, not a symbol of one. Same size ladder as before, because the Dock is where this gets decided.

01Meter Recommended

A VU meter, cream face in a machined bezel, needle deflected into the useful part of the scale with the red zone above it. Ivory-and-red is the most recognisable instrument face there is — you read it as a meter before you read it as an icon.

It is the app's own signature element, already on screen every time you record. And the pale cream face is genuinely unusual in a Dock full of saturated gradients, so it stands out by being quiet.

At 32 the needle survives because it is a thick diagonal, not a hairline. At 16 you get cream, a dark stroke and a red hint — still legible as a dial.

Verdict — distinctive, honest, and holds its shape

02Lamp Maximum restraint

One record lamp, seated in a bezel on an aluminium plate. The whole idea of the app reduced to its single accent colour: this thing is listening.

Unbeatable at small sizes — a circle inside a square never breaks. It is also the most Braun answer available, and it matches the lamp already lit in the transport deck.

The risk is category collision. A red circle is what a great many recording apps reach for, so it leans on the bezel and the brushed plate to say which one this is — detail that mostly disappears by 32.

Verdict — flawless craft, least ownable

03Key The gesture

The thing you actually do: one key, held. A chamfered aluminium cap on a dark chassis, lit lamp inset in the face, seated slightly proud of its well.

This is the only direction that depicts the interaction rather than the machine, which is a real argument for it — the app is a key you hold, not a meter you watch.

It is also the busiest. Two rectangles inside a rectangle is a weak silhouette, and by 32 the chamfer that makes it a physical key has flattened into a grey slab.

Verdict — best concept, weakest silhouette

04Grille Most unusual

A microphone grille with one perforation lit. Eight dull aluminium holes and a red centre — the one that is listening.

The most distinctive of the four. Nothing else in a Dock is a perforated plate, and the single lit hole carries the idea without a literal microphone anywhere in it.

Also the most fragile. It depends on you resolving nine separate elements, so it is really a large-size mark; by 32 the perforations merge and by 16 it is a red dot in grey fog.

Verdict — strongest at 512, weakest at 32

My pick: 01, the Meter

It is the only one that is this app rather than any recording app. The lamp is more perfectly crafted and the grille is more unusual, but a red dot could belong to anything, and the grille stops working at exactly the size the Dock uses most.

The meter earns its place three ways: it is already the app's most memorable element, ivory-and-red reads as an instrument instantly, and a pale cream icon is genuinely rare among Dock icons — it stands out by being the quiet one.

If you want the idea and not the machine

03 is the one I would argue for against myself. Murmur is a key you hold, not a meter you watch, and no other icon on the shelf depicts its own gesture. It needs redrawing at a steeper angle with a heavier chamfer to fix the silhouette — say the word and I will.
